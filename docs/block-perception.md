# Current-world perception and block-property audit

Status: first runtime perception slice implemented, **not complete human-like perception**. Resume run restored compilation and all configured checks at turn 3; the later tree-memory connection has not been verified by configured checks. The slice adds a fresh-eye 120-degree geometric view cone, 16m limit and 100,000 ray-cell budget per look direction, a bounded eight-direction look-around frame (four bearings at the bot's own pitch plus the same four tilted 45 degrees down) and exposed-face sampling for block targets, filters native block/resource/entity/tree observations, recognizes all air variants and keeps optical passage through glass separate from collision. Unknown optical materials and fluid context stop rays. Lighting, fluid attenuation, contextual shape/hazard completeness, budgets/performance evidence and regression verification remain pending. Occupied body/support cells are current contact geometry for stance validation; they cannot disclose an unseen log. No full gameplay success is claimed.

## Required behavior

Block positions are mutable. Historical observations must never become authoritative current geometry. Re-read current server-fed state before a candidate is offered and immediately before each movement, mining, pickup, placement or interaction substep. Packet updates invalidate dependent plans; unloaded, hidden or stale positions become unknown. Forgetting a chunk or changing worlds invalidates its observations. A remembered tree or destination may only motivate looking again, and must not authorize an action or be reported as currently visible.

Separate the complete server-fed chunk cache from the bot's permitted perceptual observation. Receiving hidden blocks in a chunk is not equivalent to seeing them. A bounded scan must use eye position, viewing direction, field of view, loaded distance, light, occlusion and visibility conditions. Turning provides a new view. Air permits continued rays up to configured view/load/work bounds, never infinite scanning. Glass can transmit sight while its collision geometry prevents passage. Water affects visibility and movement independently; no universal invented water-distance constant. Hidden targets must not leak into model observations, tree candidates or resource summaries. Sample budgets and unfinished scans must be observable and benchmarked.

Use separate queries for optical visibility, swept player-body collision, standing support, interaction ray/reach, fluids and hazards. Neither `is_air`, full collision, light propagation, render-face culling nor the label `Visual` alone answers all these questions. Open doors, slabs, stairs, panes, fences, waterlogged blocks and entity-dependent surfaces require their actual state and context. Check the destination, transition volume and retreat again when conditions change. Unknown rules conservatively block the affected action, with a reason; they do not silently become air or safe support.

## Concrete findings

1. Pinned `azalea-physics/src/clip.rs` maps `BlockShapeType::Visual` to collision shape and explicitly documents the visual implementation as missing. It cannot be adopted as faithful human optical perception.
2. Pinned `BlockState::is_air` only recognizes normal air. The registry AIR tag also contains cave air and void air. Existing callers need an audit; empty collision also does not mean safe air.
3. Azalea's `BlockBehavior` exposes only friction, jump factor, destroy time, explosion resistance, correct-tool requirement and forced solidity. Vanilla additionally defines speed factor, bounce, replaceability, lighting, occlusion, liquid/fire behavior, spawn/suffocation/view predicates, dynamic shapes, update behavior and other fields. Defaults must not be invented for unavailable facts.
4. Vanilla `WaterFogEnvironment.setupFog` reads environment start/end attributes and scales by the local player's water vision. Sight through water depends on context. Optical rules need a documented approximation or actual renderer-derived implementation and validation.
5. Vanilla glass registration configures no occlusion plus view/suffocation predicates separately from collision. These are separate systems, not a single transparent/walkable flag.
6. The source exposes entity-context collision and behavior overrides, for example powder snow. Generic precomputed shapes alone do not prove safe traversal for every block.

## Remaining semantic audit and implementation

For every block/state, resolve inherited and overridden behavior plus tags, loot/recipe conditions and data-pack changes. Required categories: geometry and contextual collision; support and movement effects; optical model/texture visibility and lighting; fluid state/flow; hazards and entity effects; breakability/tool/enchantment/drop rules; placement/replacement/survival; use/container/redstone actions; gravity, growth, neighbour and scheduled/random updates. Some are functions of world, time, entity, equipment or server data, not constant properties.

The catalogue marks these unresolved categories explicitly. It must not be described as having read, verified or implemented every Minecraft behavior. Pin sources, attach source and confidence per resolved rule, and make coverage fail when a new registry state is introduced without a classification or explicit unknown. Server plugins/resource packs can change behavior; observed authoritative results still decide success.

Before resuming gameplay expansion, implement a shared current-state/perception boundary and replace the existing air/support shortcuts in both observation and action feasibility. Preserve existing wood work as unfinished. Required tests include: wood replaced by stone/air/water; wall hiding resource; visible resource behind glass but unreachable interaction; air/cave air/void air; water and fog context; open/closed doors; slabs/stairs/panes; waterlogged geometry; changing support during jump/retreat; chunk unload and world reset; bounded scans and no hidden-state leakage. Native UI and live evidence must follow implementation, not substitute for these tests.

## Shared-frame work budget and measurement

One current immutable visible frame supplies trees, resources, landmarks, waypoints and block facts; no block contents survive into the next frame. Scan offsets are fixed version-independent geometry, ordered nearest-first. Entity observation has its own 4,096-cell budget so block scanning cannot consume it. Terrain remains bounded at 100,000 cell reads per look direction and omitted cells stay unknown.

The frame is a bounded look-around, not one cone: four bearings at the bot's own pitch, then the same four bearings tilted 45 degrees down, because the ground a stance stands on lies roughly 65 degrees below the eye and falls outside a horizontal 120-degree cone. Each direction has its own 100,000-cell budget, so a frame stops after 8 directions or 800,000 reads at the very most, and the observation reports how many directions were sampled, how many reads they cost and whether the frame was truncated. The four tilted directions are the reason the frame costs about four to five times the reads of one 120-degree cone: a single cone is one `Sight` scan bounded by the same 100,000-cell budget, while the frame allows that budget in each of its eight directions. The observation runs once per decision turn, not per tick.

A block target is sampled at the centre of the exposed face that points most toward the eye, and a face only counts as exposed when the cell across it is see-through. Aiming at a cell's centre instead makes the ray meet the ground surface in front of a buried block, which left the ground under every stance outside the bot's own contact column unobserved and made every harvest route unverifiable. Execution aims at that same face: `wood_tree::can_mine` casts its stance ray at the exposed-face point, not at the block centre, so a stance the observation verified is not refused later because a centre ray clips the corner of the block beside the bot. A centre ray did exactly that in `runs/level1-live/live-dt.json` event 20, where the bot coasted 0.20 blocks past its stance centre next to the block above the wall beside it, and the attempt died with "Gather mining stance, support or sightline changed" while the block's face was open the whole time.

Native observations now pass the current tree scan through `TreeMemory`. The memory retains tree identity, unresolved known logs and access/support dependencies when a cell leaves sight or a frame is unavailable. Reconciliation removes a remembered log only when the current visible frame shows a different block at that exact cell. Spawn epochs or a changed dimension reset the memory. Remembered coordinates can suppress a harvest to protect unresolved access, but cannot create a harvest option: every `next` target and route originates in the current `inspect` pass using visible cells and current contact geometry for the occupied stance. The public tree log list is filtered to exact currently visible wood (or cleared when the frame is unavailable), while status can describe the count of unresolved history. A scan that stops at the 24-tree cap keeps the harvests of the trees it enumerated and reports the components it never reached in their status instead; a dropped component is not an enumerated tree's own loss. Only wood beyond the 128-log limit of a tree's own component counts as that tree's capacity loss: the tree is marked, its own verified steps are withheld, and the memory keeps the loss for the whole epoch with an explicit capacity status until the world epoch resets, because lost dependencies cannot be safely reconstructed. Ordinary partial visibility does not trigger this block. Hidden wood is not a currently visible resource or a positive action target. Execution still rechecks the target and movement geometry against current server-fed state. Native regressions cover both scan limits; live verification remains pending.

Tree views now serialize a bounded aggregate `diagnostics` summary for blocked harvests. `unknown_visibility` is true for a scan boundary, missing sampled sightline cell, or unresolved remembered wood. `stance_or_route` counts currently visible targets without a verified reachable stance and clear ray, including known ray obstructions. `support_or_upper_log` counts current scan options withheld to preserve route support or higher observed wood. `memory_veto` counts current options removed by remembered access dependencies or unresolved higher wood. The blocked status also prints these categories. No rejected route or hidden coordinate is serialized in the diagnostics; counts are limited by the eight-component, 128-log scan and three options per component. A count is a conservative explanation of why this bounded pass found no candidate, not a proof that a route or harvest is impossible. Older serialized views default to an empty summary, and capacity loss remains a separate epoch-wide block.

The deterministic `cargo run --example perception-profile` diagnostic measures native ray queries against a synthetic plane, not a live-server latency claim. Before the shared frame: 94,130 reads and 11.08–11.48ms over five runs. With the shared frame and one look direction: 74,152 reads, exhausted false and the nearby landmark retained; first run 23.54ms includes one-time offset ordering, subsequent runs 10.08–10.77ms. Without the shared frame the same probe spends the full 100,000 reads and reports exhausted with the nearby landmark lost (11.59–12.67ms). Exposed-face sampling raised the per-direction reads from the earlier 51,903 because a solid cell now costs up to three neighbour lookups to find its exposed face. A full eight-direction frame cost 460,644 reads over the reconstructed turn-23 geometry that `tests/perception_lookaround.rs` builds from the recorded positions, well under the 800,000 bound. That figure was measured over that geometry; the test pins the bound — all eight directions, a nonzero read count and an unexhausted budget — not the exact number. In the open-air scene, the previous repeated scan exhausted 100,000 reads and lost a nearby cell; the shared-frame scenario retains near observations. Run both `--open-air` and `--open-air --shared` to compare. Live adapter profiling and regression tests remain required.

## Module and version direction

User steering: consider reusable modules and eventual plugins while implementing; do not build a plugin framework now. Keep observation freshness, bounded work, plan/attempt lifecycle and outcome validation general. Minecraft connection/protocol actions belong in the adapter. Block/recipe semantics belong behind a version-specific rules boundary with explicit source/version and unsupported cases. The present `perception` module still imports Azalea and is a Minecraft implementation, not yet an engine-neutral interface. Avoid duplicating it for mining, construction or future goals. New data can eventually be supplied by version modules; protocol and behavior changes still require code and regression checks. No plugin system has been implemented or promised as a universal configuration-only upgrade.

Native entity work now enumerates at most 256 actors from the nine adjacent indexed chunks rather than sorting every known actor. Hostile actors anywhere in that bounded sample remain available to the safety policy even when 32 ordinary display landmarks have filled the list. Index or visibility-budget truncation is an explicit observation flag that blocks productive wood work; it does not prove the area safe. Mining independently requires the exact target to be visible from the current eye before queueing a forced packet. Live adapter latency and lighting/fluid context remain unverified.

Wood movement rechecks every next-step swept body cell immediately before walking or jumping, including the additional ceiling cells for a one-block height change and an airborne cancellation tick. It separately rechecks landing support. The bot turns toward each cell for a fresh bounded `Sight` query; only its occupied body/support column may use current contact geometry. Unknown, unloaded, occluded or non-air clearance and unverified support pause the step and release walking/jumping. Loaded checks over the remaining route still reject changed endpoints and jump clearance, but cannot authorize the next step. Focused regressions cover changed landing support, hidden ascent headroom and changed clearance while airborne. This is a conservative cell-volume guard, not a complete continuous collision or hazard simulation.
